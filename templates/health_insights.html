Know your health better

Quick facts about different parts of your health.

❤️

Heart

A normal resting heart rate for adults is usually 60–100 beats per minute.

Regular physical activity can help support cardiovascular health.

Cholesterol and triglycerides are commonly monitored through blood tests.

🫁

Lungs

Your lungs continuously bring oxygen into your body and remove carbon dioxide.

Regular movement can support healthy breathing and lung function.

Avoiding smoking and second-hand smoke is important for lung health.

🧠

Brain

Your brain controls thinking, memory, movement, emotions, and many automatic body functions.

Sleep plays an important role in memory and concentration.

Regular physical activity and mental stimulation can support overall brain health.

🫘

Kidneys

Your kidneys filter waste and extra fluid from the blood to help maintain balance in the body.

Staying adequately hydrated supports normal kidney function.

Creatinine is one commonly measured marker related to kidney function.

🩸

Blood

Blood carries oxygen and nutrients while helping protect the body from infection.

A Complete Blood Count can measure red blood cells, white blood cells, hemoglobin, and platelets.

Hemoglobin helps carry oxygen throughout the body.

😴

Sleep

Most adults generally need around 7–9 hours of sleep each night.

A consistent sleep schedule can support better sleep quality.

Good sleep supports memory, concentration, and daily energy.

💧

Hydration

Water is essential for temperature regulation, digestion, circulation, and many other body functions.

Your daily fluid needs can vary depending on activity, climate, and individual factors.

Fruits and vegetables can also contribute to your fluid intake.

🥗

Nutrition

A balanced diet provides the body with essential nutrients, vitamins, minerals, protein, and fiber.

Whole grains, fruits, vegetables, and protein-rich foods can contribute to a balanced diet.

Small, sustainable dietary changes are often easier to maintain.

🩺

Blood Sugar

Blood glucose is one of the common measurements found in laboratory reports.

Fasting blood glucose is measured after a period without food.

Your report's reference range provides context for the result.

🧬

Cholesterol

Cholesterol is a substance your body needs, but different cholesterol levels can provide useful health information.

Lipid profiles commonly include total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, and triglycerides.

Tracking values across reports can help you see changes over time.

🛡️

Immunity

Your immune system helps identify and defend the body against harmful organisms.

Adequate sleep, nutrition, physical activity, and hygiene support overall health.

White blood cells are an important part of the immune system.

🏃

Physical Activity

Regular physical activity can support cardiovascular, muscular, and mental wellbeing.

Walking, cycling, swimming, and other activities can contribute to an active lifestyle.

Even small amounts of movement throughout the day can be useful.

🫀

Liver

The liver performs many important functions including processing nutrients and helping remove substances from the blood.

Liver function tests may include measurements such as ALT, AST, bilirubin, and other markers.

Test results should always be interpreted using the relevant reference range and clinical context.

🍎

Digestion

Your digestive system breaks food down so your body can absorb nutrients and energy.

Fiber, fluids, and a varied diet can support digestive health.

Eating slowly and maintaining regular meal patterns may also support comfortable digestion.

📋

Regular Checkups

Regular health checkups can help you keep track of important measurements and changes over time.

Keeping previous reports makes it easier to compare your health measurements over time.

MedTrack helps organize your reports so changes are easier to see.
